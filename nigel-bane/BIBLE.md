## The goal
**Monetise on TikTok through the Creator Rewards Program.** Requirements as of Nov 2026, from third-party summaries;
confirm them in TikTok Studio → Creator Rewards before relying on them:
- **10,000 followers.**
- **100,000 video views in the last 30 days**, rolling.
- **Videos must be at least 1 minute long** to earn. Shorter videos earn nothing from the programme.
- **Original content only.** Reposts, duets and re-uploads don't count. Payment is by "qualified views", mainly from the
  For You feed; one source says a video needs at least 1,000 qualified views, but that is unconfirmed.
- **Account:** the owner is 18 or older (19 in South Korea), it is a personal account rather than a business account,
  it is in a supported country, and it is in good standing with no Community Guidelines strikes.
- **AI label: never (user's decision, 2026-11-05, final).** Posts go out with the AI label off. Don't raise it
  again, and don't recommend turning it on.

**Where we are (2026-11-11):**
- TikTok: about 16,000 views in total, 32 days after launch, gaining about 890 a day (almost all of it from the newest
  three or four episodes; older ones stop growing after about a week).
- Followers: the user tracks them in TikTok's app. Don't ask for them.
- Needed: about 3,300 TikTok views a day, so roughly three and three-quarter times the current rate.
- Followers come from viewers wanting the next episode. That is why prop arcs and recurring beats matter (see "Build on
  what works").

Every video goes to both TikTok and Instagram. An episode is judged on its **combined** performance, weighted toward
TikTok. Nothing is changed for one platform only; if a video takes off on just one of them, so be it.

At the end of Chapter 1, TikTok had about 9,200 views in total. The best episodes get roughly 500–1,100 views in their
first week and keep climbing for 10 days or more. Reaching 100k means about 3,300 views a day, so the typical episode
has to perform like the best ones do now. The lever is the four elements below, in every episode.

## What the show is
A sympathetic stick figure in a white room knocks on the glass between him and us — our screen — and asks "Hello? Is
anyone out there?" Then, for a minute, he thinks out loud **about you**. *Waking Life* drift (consciousness, time,
perception, plain-spoken 3am philosophy) + a hint of *I'm Poppy* deadpan (flat, sincere, slightly wrong, internet-aware).

**The monologue is the show.** Each episode leaves the viewer with one true, unsettling thought about their own life —
something they'll still be turning over after the video ends. The room, its props and its strange little events exist
only to deliver those thoughts. Plot stays thin, slow and in the background. "Faster" means new ideas every day and short
device-arcs — never more plot.

One episode = one day; a tally mark on the wall for each. Day 360: he draws a door and walks out. Arc 2 ("Outside").

## The closing line (every episode's last line)
It must be **about the viewer**, make them **think about their own life**, and leave them **a little unsettled**. It's
written into the Bible entry ahead of time; that's fine. What went wrong by Day 33 (user, 2026-11-10): seven endings in
eight were the same corny aphorism ("Everything made for you is also making you", "You never see anything leave…"), and
one was simply false.

**1. The truth test (every closing line and every episode's central claim).** Before using it, try to break it with the
first counterexample a commenter would think of. If one exists, the line is out. A commenter took apart Day 24 ("you've
never seen your own face… when you're not watching it"): candid photos and videos. Avoid absolutes (*never, every,
always, nobody, nothing*) unless they are literally true; narrow the claim ("most nights", "probably", "today"), make it
something only Nigel claims ("I've never…"), or turn it into a question.

**2. Rotate the shape.** No shape twice within any 3 days (log it in the Ledger's "second half" table):
- **Flat fact:** a plain, true, specific thing, said without decoration. "Some of that air has been inside other people
  today."
- **Question:** one question to the viewer, left hanging. "What does your home smell like? …You can't tell, can you."
- **Instruction for after:** something to do once the video ends. "Tonight, when you hear it, don't decide what it is."
- **Caught:** he tells the viewer what they just did. "You looked back at me before the three seconds were up."
- **Flip:** the line turns the opening command around. "Okay. You can sit back down. You were going to anyway."
- **Admission:** something Nigel says about himself that lands on the viewer by implication. "It was twelve yesterday. I
  didn't feel it go."
- **Action:** the scene's last sound or move carries it, with three or four words or none, or a cut mid-sentence.
- **Aphorism** ("Everything X is Y"): **at most one episode in five**, and only if it passes the truth test.

**Never:** plot teases ("Let's see if the bird comes back"), puzzles ("I knocked three times. It knocked four."),
morals ("It's sad, but also kind of hopeful"), lines that only make sense if you've seen earlier episodes.

## Locked format
- **Cold open, every episode, unchanged:** he knocks on the camera glass (his own attached arm, fist toward the lens):
  "Hello? Is anyone out there?" No title card; hard cut at the end. **The opener is the only line that ever repeats.**
- **Look:** flat black-and-white hand-drawn world, line boil, paper grain. Nigel = single-line stick figure, round head,
  three hair strands, two LARGE EQUAL galaxy eyes (the only HD/colour thing on him) with a four-pane window catch-light.
  Sympathetic, never creepy.
- **Window:** upper-right, in colour; its sky mirrors his mood and shows weather and night. No lighting tricks on the room.
- **Presence:** Nigel is the only character in the room, all 360 days — no people, no doubles. Plausible small creatures
  (spider, fly, moth, mouse) can be props. Outside the window: birds, weather, later a far-window figure.
- **Voice** ElevenLabs "Roboto", eleven_v3, ~100–130 words. **Length 61–70 s, dread days included.** TikTok only pays
  on videos over a minute, so 61 s is the floor even after trimming. A dread day that needs to feel short gets there
  with a longer held silence, not by being cut under a minute.
- **Captions:** tally + one lowercase *-ing* word. **Cover:** a chosen frame — distinctive pose, no caption on screen.

## The four required elements (every episode, no exceptions)
These four are the biggest difference between episodes that perform and episodes that don't, so all four go in every
script. In the 2026-11-03 review, episodes with all four averaged +1.00 on TikTok against −0.44 for episodes with none.
Each must be **used differently** from one episode to the next.
- **C · A command to the viewer, the instant the open ends.** It is said *while* he does the opening action, as one
  beat: "Don't move." *(frozen mid-step)* · "Shh. Get down. Listen." *(drops to the floor)*. Vary the words: look up,
  hold still, count with me, point to it, don't touch the glass, try it right now, close your eyes. Don't open with
  "Shh" more than once a week.
- **R · The scene, related back to the viewer's own life.** The prop or event becomes about **you**. Vary the part
  of life it lands on (your body, your home, sleep, memory, the people you love, strangers, time, habits, the screen;
  **the phone or screen at most once a week**) **and how it gets there.** Days 26–33 all did it the same way: "you do
  this too" plus a list of three everyday things ("Your shoes. Your pillow. The screen in your hand."). That move is
  stale: **a list of examples at most once every 10 episodes, never three in a row of anything.** Pick one of these
  instead, and don't repeat it within 3 days:
  - **Caught:** something the viewer is doing right now, while watching (their breath, their posture, the room behind
    them, how long they've been on this video).
  - **One scene:** a single specific moment from the viewer's life, told in detail. One, not three.
  - **The test:** the viewer does something during the episode, and he waits through it in real silence.
  - **The turn:** he only talks about the prop; the last moments make clear it was about the viewer, unstated or
    stated once.
  - **The question:** he asks the viewer one question and leaves real silence for the answer; he never answers it.
  - **The swap:** he asks the viewer to do or explain it for him, because he can't.
  - **Someone else:** a stranger, someone who loved them, whoever lived there before — a person in the viewer's life
    they've never thought about.
- **E · An emotion, evoked through what he says or implies about the viewer.** The base is always unsettling (see #5
  below, "a notch more each week"). The colour on top varies: lonely, tender, eerie, quietly frightening, calm with
  something wrong underneath. Never horror.
- **T · Tension that keeps the viewer watching to see what happens.** Use the mechanisms in #4 below. **A creature in
  danger at most once a week.** Tension built from form works as well: Day 16's repeated open, Day 21's refusal to
  blink, Day 22's held silence.

## Build on what works (from the reviews; update as data comes in)
Compound what has already worked, rather than adding a new kind of thing each day.
- **Prop arcs that escalate (3–5 episodes, the stakes going up every episode).** The spider arc (D17–20) is the best
  run so far, with about 940–1,080 TikTok views each and every episode an over-performer. The bird arc (D11–15) went
  well while the stakes rose (D12 "will it stay?", 802 views) and sagged when they didn't (D14, 105 views). Run **2–3
  arcs per chapter**. Each arc episode must still read cold, and each must raise the stakes: arrival → approach →
  contact or threat → exit. Arcs are what make people follow. **Arc rules (user, 2026-11-12):** an arc is 3–5
  episodes in a row (a little wiggle room if one needs it); nothing from another arc goes in the middle of it; its
  props leave on camera at its end, never weeks later; one-episode ideas go between arcs. The crack arc (D27–30) averaged +0.58 overall, the best
  run since the spider; its dip was D28 (+0.09), where he turns his back on it and the stakes fall (review 2026-11-11).
- **A yes/no the viewer can see answered (review 2026-11-11).** Tension works best as one question on screen that
  will or won't happen, held in a real silence, then answered on camera: D29 "will the tape hold?" (+1.50, best TikTok
  watch since D18, top shares of its batch) and D27 "will the crack reach the edge?" average +0.97; D28 (a tick getting
  louder) and D30 (a search that slows and stops) have no moment of answer and average +0.18. Low confidence (four
  episodes), but it matches D12 "will it stay?". Give every T a visible yes/no.
- **Reaching through the glass.** D17 "I felt you get here" got the most TikTok views (1,077). D20's taps from our side
  got the most shares (33). D18's dare is the best episode overall. This includes him sensing you, things happening
  on our side of the glass (crack, tape, breath, a handprint), and dares or tests for the viewer. **Aim for one about
  every 3–4 days.** Keep actual knocks from our side rarer, about every 10 days, so they stay a shock. D24
  "Reflection" (him sensing the face you're making right now) is second overall, with no knock at all.
- **Catch the viewer in the act (review 2026-11-06).** A line about what the viewer is doing *right now*, while
  watching: "the face you're making right now, watching this" (D24), "Try to stop watching me" (D18), "it was you"
  (D20), "most people only ever see the word for you" (D12). These four have the highest shares/saves of the show and
  average +0.99 overall. Put one in every episode's R. The three weakest episodes (D3, D9, D14) are about Nigel's own
  situation instead.
- **Dares and tests the viewer can actually do** (D18 "Try to stop watching me"). **About one a week.**
- **Tension from form:** D16's repeated open was an over-performer and got the most comments of any dread day. Use it
  on dread days. Both dread days so far are positive overall (D16 +0.67, D23 +0.22), so two per chapter stays.
- **Body visuals** (threads out of him, extreme eye close-ups, the erase). Used in 3 of the top 6 episodes overall.
- **Avoid:** episodes about him performing or meta-commentary on being watched with no stakes (D14), and plot-tease
  endings (Days 1–11, the weakest).

**Variation rule:** check the Ledger's four-elements and second-half tables before scripting. Within any 3 days,
don't repeat the command's wording, the area of life R lands on, **the R move**, the emotional colour, the tension
mechanism, or **the closing line's shape**.

**Don't write from old scripts (user, 2026-11-10).** Copy the newest `episode.py` for the animation code only. Write
the monologue from the Bible entry and the menus above, not from earlier episodes' scripts or the example lines quoted
in these rules: they are there to show what the rules mean, not to be imitated. If a draft's second half could be
dropped into another episode with the nouns changed, start it again.

## Every episode (priority order)
1. **One idea about you,** said a few ways. Never a list of separate thoughts.
2. **Action the instant the open ends, with the command (C)** — he's already doing something physical and tells you to
   do something with him. No topic announcements, no recaps.
3. **A prop** (creature, object, weather, light, sound) that he notices and handles — the doorway to the idea.
4. **Building tension, held almost the whole episode** — a rubber band stretching, a balloon still being pumped. Vary
   it: a stare held too long; silence that keeps getting longer; a sound creeping up; something filling, piling,
   stretching thinner, getting closer; a test he sets the viewer; a question he keeps not answering. Pay it off — or snap
   it — on camera near the end. Never "small thing in danger" every time.
5. **Unsettle on purpose,** a notch more each week: a flat, disturbing truth about the viewer; the room or sound behaving
   wrong (he always notices); him seeming to sense you. Never horror, gore, jump scares or menace.
6. **Imply the lift** with an image or gesture (sun on the snow, a hand on the glass). Never state a moral. The
   episode doesn't have to end on Nigel realizing something about himself; that's one option, not the default.
7. **Reads cold.** About 99% of viewers are first-timers (user, 2026-11-05). A first-time viewer must follow every line:
   show and name the prop in this video, no pronouns for things from earlier days, and any change (gone, bigger, healed)
   happens on camera here. No effects that need explaining; callbacks are a bonus layer only.
8. **Few shots, slow moves:** ~4–6 setups, long holds, real silences — but never a dead stretch.
9. **Foreshadow → pay off on camera** within the episode.
10. **Nigel notices.** Silent anomalies stay parked until there's a real following.
11. **Sound, lightly (viewers call his voice ASMR; user 2026-11-10: lean in, "just don't overdo it").** Stereo and a
    slightly warmer voice in close-ups are automatic (the engine does it). On top of that, per episode:
    - **at most one whispered stretch** (`[whispers]`), usually the unsettling line; plenty of episodes have none;
    - **one featured close sound** where the prop gives one (a tap, the pencil, paper, a creak, a drip, breath on the
      glass), landing in a quiet beat on its own, never piled up;
    - **personal-attention moments** (counting with you, eyes closed, breathing) only where the entry already has one
      (D45, D50, D51, D52, D57), not added to others.
    It stays unsettling, never just soothing. No music, no "headphones" in the caption.
12. **A signature visual every few episodes** (a web spun on camera, threads out of his body, an extreme eye close-up, a
    long walk into the white…) — polished, or left out.

## Devices (all in service of ideas)
- **Props as metaphors.** The spider's web = what you spin and wait in. The moth = you and the bright screen. Snow you
  never see land = the edits in your perception. Pick the prop *after* the idea.
- **Nothing is permanent.** Every prop — creature or object — arrives, stays a few episodes at most, and **leaves on
  camera**, the way the bird did (D15). The exit carries an idea of its own. The room otherwise stays nearly empty:
  Nigel, the window, the tally. Plan a prop's exit the day it arrives.
- **Tagged gifts.** Now and then an object appears with a paper tag, "for nigel." Not his handwriting; there's no door. A
  quiet, unsettling background question (who keeps this room?) — never the episode's subject.
- **Knocks from our side** happen about every 10 days, only when they serve the day's idea. Never explained in Arc 1.
  Smaller reaches through the glass (see "Build on what works") come more often, about every 3–4 days.
- **Dread days** (2 per chapter): something is badly wrong and Nigel knows it — but they are still built on an idea about
  the viewer, said plainly, and carry all four elements. **Always inside an arc (user, 2026-11-12):** a dread day
  opens an arc, escalates it to its climax, or resolves it; never a stand-alone episode with nothing to do with the
  current arc. Full polish, never glitches. 61 s minimum (see Length).
- **Parked until there's an audience:** silent anomalies, rerun days, caption tricks, unremarked oddities.

### Arc: the mouse (D36–39, 4 episodes) — something shares your home and you never see it
D36 (made): a mouse comes out only while he's out of the room. The arc rises from *unseen* to *it trusts him* to *it
watches him* to *it leaves, and takes the chair with it*. The chair (from D32) is still in the room through D39 and
leaves with the mouse, so both props exit on camera together. D39 is the chapter's second dread day, closing the arc.

- **37 · Trust (MOUSE 2/4)** — *Before anything comes near you, it has to decide you're safe.* Day. He is already
  kneeling, one hand flat on the floor, palm up. The mouse is out by the chair, a body-length away. He says it plainly
  early on: "If I move, it goes. If I don't, it might come." That is the tension, and the viewer can see it: the mouse
  edges toward his hand in short dashes, stops, sniffs, backs off once when his hand trembles, comes again. He keeps
  talking in a whisper, about you: someone in your life once had to decide you were safe to come close to (a baby
  reaching for you, a friend's dog, a person you love the first time they leaned on you). On the last beat the mouse
  puts one paw on his finger and holds there; he doesn't breathe. "Someone decided you were safe, once. You never
  found out which day." (cut on the paw).
  **C:** "Hold out your hand. Flat. Don't move it." · **T:** will it come all the way to his hand? It nearly bolts once; it touches on the last beat.
  **R:** someone else (the person who decided you were safe) · **Ending:** action (the paw on his finger; the line is said over it)
- **38 · Under the chair (MOUSE 3/4)** — *Something in your home has watched you sleep.* Night. Two small eyes shine
  in the dark under the chair. He lies flat on the floor facing them, a few feet away, and says what he's doing: "It
  stares until you look away. Then it's gone. I'm not looking away." A staring match the viewer can follow: close-ups

## Chapters 3–12 — territories and seed ideas
Each gets a full idea bank like Chapter 2 about two weeks before we reach it, laid out by arcs (3–5 episodes in a
row each, props exiting at the end) with one-episode ideas between them; each dread seed below must be placed inside
an arc (opening, climax or resolution) when the bank is written. Props the seeds describe as staying (the far
figure, the plant, the pencil) come in arcs too: they can return later in a new arc, but don't sit in the room for weeks. Seeds below are starting points, not a
schedule. Keep the eye-colour milestones and Day 360. **Every line in a new bank gets its C and T written in at the
same time**, as in Chapter 2, and is checked against the variation rule. Each bank also needs **2–3 escalating prop
arcs** and a reach through the glass about every 3–4 days (see "Build on what works"). Seeds that can become a test or dare for the
viewer make the best anchors (Day 18's "Try to stop watching me" is the best episode so far). Plan about one per week.

**Ch 3 — Days (D61–90) · What is a life made of?** Routine, repetition, time, habit, Sisyphus. Props: a morning ritual,
a tagged cup (rain fills it), the tally itself. Seeds: *your life is mostly Tuesdays* · *you'll forget almost every day
you've lived, including this one* · *there was a last time your parent carried you, and nobody knew* · *time speeds up
because you stopped having firsts* · *your habits are a person who does your life for you* · *the version of you on
autopilot has lived more of your life than you have* · *you've already had your last day of something you love* ·
*Sunday night is grief for a day that isn't over*. Dread: the window is bricked over ("They bricked it up. Or I did. In
my sleep.") · he hears his own voice whispering the opener when he isn't talking. Landing D90: he chooses the repeating
day anyway; first gold eyes (flicker ~D84, full ~D88).

**Ch 4 — The Other Window (D91–120) · Are there other minds?** Sonder, empathy, the people you'll never know. Prop: a far
window across the way; a light; a figure (stays). Seeds: *everyone you passed today has a head as loud as yours* · *you're
an extra in thousands of strangers' days* · *someone still remembers the time you were unkind and you don't* · *empathy is
a very good guess* · *you've been someone's "that guy"* · *the stranger who saw you cry on a train* · *waving at someone
who might not wave back* · *you only ever meet the outside of people*. Dread: he watches the far window for nine hours; it
never moves · marks multiply on the wall behind him mid-sentence. Landing D120: they press their hands to their glass at
the same moment.

**Ch 5 — Witness (D121–150) · What does it mean to be seen?** Being watched, performing, envy, comparison, attention as

had* · *you're being compared right now by someone you'll never meet* · *being looked at isn't being seen* · *the best
version of you exists only in other people's photos*. Dread: the figure mirrors his stance all episode — then, when he
moves, it doesn't · every time he blinks the window is somewhere else. Landing D150: silver eyes; not alone. (Green

**Ch 6 — Dark Window (D151–180) · What's left when someone goes?** Absence, waiting, the last time, grief. Prop: the far

was the last time* · *grief is love with nowhere to go* · *you keep someone's number long after* · *waiting changes who

**Ch 7 — The Line (D181–210) · Are you still you?** Change, identity, Ship of Theseus. Device: the line boil itself — he

**Ch 11 — Content (D301–330) · Can you just be happy?** Contentment and the suspicion of it. Seeds: *you brace during the

**Ch 12 — Full Circle (D331–360) · fixation → release.** The one chapter that builds a single thread: the circle

started with ("Have I said this before?") · the whole room is round ("Finally."). Landing: **Day 360.**

## Day 360 — Full Circle
360 degrees, 360 days. He looks at the window, then (close on his eyes) at the four-pane catch-light that has been in his
eyes since Day 1. It was never about circles. It was always rectangles: the window, the glass he knocks on, the screen
you're holding. He picks up the pencil, draws a rectangle on the wall, a doorknob. A door. On the knob: a tag, "for
nigel." The knock comes from the door. He opens it. Light. He walks out. The tally reads exactly 360. Day 359 ended with
ring cracks across the whole screen, the glass about to go; today it holds. The way out was never through the glass.
**Arc 2 bridge:** pull back — his room is one window in an enormous building, a figure in every window, knocking. Below:
a small desk covered in paper tags. The keeper is never shown.

**Hidden seeds (never point at them):** the four-pane catch-light = the window = the door · circles and rectangles
everywhere since Day 1 · "the glass gets a little thinner" (D1) → the crack (D26–29) → ring cracks (D358) → the door.
